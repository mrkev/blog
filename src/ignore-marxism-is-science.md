# Marxism is science and USA lost all its scientists

I briefly talked about capitalism with a friend, and mentioned that I think China is better at it than the USA in spite of being communist. I don't think this is a stretch. Quantitatively, there's plenty of arguments for this if one looks at growth charts, but I won't make the argument for that in that post.

I think what's interesting is how this could even come to be. The view many people hold is that capitalism and socialism are opposites, enemies. Picking one is rejecting the other. I think there's more complexity to this. TODO

## 1. Marx was a scientist

First, I have to express that in my eyes Marx primarily a philosopher, a scientist. Sure, he was also involved in politics, and edited a radical newspaper, etc. but I think one would be best to understand him as a scientist first and foremost.

When we talk about science we usually think about the natural sciences: physics, chemistry, biology, etc. When we think of technology, we usually think of the application of these natural sciences; we apply new insights in physics to engineer better rockets, in chemistry to create new materials, in biology to create new cures to diseases, etc. We shouldn't forget the humanities are sciences though, and they equivalently have associated technological development.

When we understand the way people think, feel and act better, we can engineer better policy, systems of government, more effective education, etc. Understanding our minds in the individual and as societies, leads to social and political technology.

Marx lived at a time when this brand new economic and political system called "capitalism" was taking the world by storm and he decided to study it. His magnum opus was _Das Kaptial_, a book trying to understand Capitalism, a study. In a similar way to how Albert Einstein developed a new theory (general relativity) to study gravity, or Copernicus developed a new theory (heliocentric) to understand the way he saw the planets move, Marx developed a new theory (historical materialism) to understand capitalism. Like other great scientists, he presented his theory, used it to explain his observations, and then made predictions based on them.

Einstein predicted black holes to great controversy. Copernicus predicted that the sun is at the center of the (then known) universe, to great controversy. Karl Marx predicted that capitalism isn't sustainable and would eventually self-destruct, which seems to still be causing controversy to this day.

## 2. Socialism is technology

Had the Catholic Church had been in the middle of a space race when Copernicus developed his heliocentric theory, they would've been cooked. They staunchly opposed heliocentrism, but scientific theories aren't a mater of opinion, they're a mater of explaining how we see the real world work. No matter how passionately they'd want to oppose heliocentrism on ideological, or personal, or dogmatic bases, when they'd launched that rocket the moon simply wouldn't've been there. At the end of the day, their dogmatism would've been their peril, a self-inflicted nerf to their own capacity to develop technology. Not accepting a correct theory is limiting oneself to work and shoot in the dark.

The United States has rejected Marxism —including not only historical materialism but derivative work, such as dialectical materialism— even though for the most part it's remained relevant because it's been useful, applicable, and seems to be correct. My study of philosophy, history, sociology, anthropology and other humanities is minimal and not fully formal, but it's been enough to realize that there often are _before Marx_ and _after Marx_ eras in many social sciences. He's considered a "founding father" of sociology, is a key figure in political science and economics, shows up often in modern anthropology and cultural studies, provides a very solid basis to understand history, etc. Marxist theory enriches and/or is crucial in all sorts of fields and applications, from philosophy to urban planning.

So what is socialism? Emotional reactions and ideological dogmas aside, it undeniably is technology. Some will argue it's good technology and others will argue it's bad technology, but manicheisms aside, it's is a result of the application of a social science, based on a theory that has proven to be very useful and quite robust.

## 3. The USA cooked itself

It is very dumb then, in my opinion, that the USA remains so staunchly afraid of socialism. It's anabaptist christianity (the Amish) remaining anti-electricity in the 21st century. It's the Catholic Church, rejecting heliocentrism. The USA has willingly destroyed its own development in social sciences and humanities on "ideological" basis and set itself technologically behind.

I'm not implying the USA should become socialist. Let it reject socialism and communism, let it try to remain capitalist forever— rejecting Marxism so thoroughly affects has only affected it's own capacity to manage the very capitalism it aims to protect. Karl Marx developed a theory to understand capitalism and concluded it's destined to collapse. Real intellectual fortitude would have the United States be foremost in the study of Marxism; swaths of sociologists, political philosophers, and historians graduating from from the top universities in the nation, understanding dialectical materialism forwards and backwards, and building a new capitalist system to survive the internal conflicts that would've spelled it's doom.

Instead we saw a witch-hunt of communists during the McCarthy era and an overall abandonment of social sciences as a whole.

Plenty has been said about China being better at capitalism than the USA— is it a surprise, when the USA threw half its tools to understand and develop it out of the window? If Marx wrote _Das Kapital_ and developed its seminal theory to understand capitalsm, is it surprising that a nation that respects and thoroughly studies Marx is the best at capitalism? The level of sophistication and complexity of the Chinese economy is a direct product of ideological developments to guide correct decision-making. China's economy, government, and social organization is and order of magnitude more complex than the USA's— and this is good for them. Technology is complexity. Behind the simplicity of a modern-day smartphone, is the insurmountable complexity of countless transistors, industrial processes, and supply chains.

---

So overall, I think the USA is cooked. For all the focus it has on intellect and promoting education in the natural sciences and engineering in its population, it also deprived its population of other sciences and other technologies which have set it behind too far to feasibly catch up. One can see it in the terrible politicians, it produces, their simplistic ideology, and the subsequent lackluster policy seemingly stuck in the last century while the rest of the world keeps moving on.
